"I had to give in. I wanted to stay alive more than I wanted to complete my mission."

Z's voice trembled. His eyes were filled with tears, carrying the weight of regret and guilt. He struggled to maintain eye contact.

"Sixteen of our men. Ten of yours. You call that a failed mission? I'd call it a massacre. You were never a proper agent, Z. You were a liability," Mizar replied.

Mizar had spent twenty years serving in the Israeli forces. He had been part of Mossad even before the organisation orchestrated the Iraq conflicts. His body carried the consequences of those years, particularly the mutilation he suffered during an Iranian airstrike. Yet, despite everything, he continued to serve as an intelligence operative.

"You can't work for an intelligence agency while secretly serving as a spy for another. It simply doesn't work that way. We knew you were an agent when we recruited you. We employed you because of it," Raaya added.

Z's hands were trembling. He kept rubbing his fingers against one another, as though trying to regain control of them. Dark circles surrounded his eyes, and his face carried the exhaustion of someone who hadn't slept properly in years.

There was something peculiar about Z. He possessed a wit and charisma that could captivate almost anyone. Outside the walls of intelligence agencies and military establishments, he could have been the most interesting person in any room he entered.

He could have been a great actor. Perhaps even a celebrated one, had he been born somewhere else. Or perhaps if he had fled his country that night.

But some people never get the luxury of choosing the lives they want.

By fourteen, Z had already been diagnosed with Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD). While other boys his age were discovering the world, Z was learning how to survive it.

Z was, in many ways, a living dead man long before he had attained manhood.

He avoided violence of all kinds. Growing up, he had witnessed death at an age when most children were still learning how to understand it.

He wasn't born in a terrorist country, but terrorism had been forced upon his country, his neighbourhood and, eventually, his childhood.

Many nights, Z cried himself to sleep with his ears covered, desperately trying to shut out the sounds of gunshots and machetes. Sometimes, even when the streets were quiet, he could still hear them.

The first major episode occurred when his friend Hassan was killed in a gang fight.

Hassan was thirteen. They had been playing hide-and-seek that afternoon. Like any other thirteen-year-old, he was more concerned about finding the perfect hiding spot than anything happening around him.

He chose his uncle's building, unaware that it stood right next to Safdar's turf.

When Hassan peeked out, hoping to catch a glimpse of the seeker, a 5.56 mm bullet struck the back of his neck.

Z was hiding near the same building.

The neighbourhood erupted into gunfire. Bullets crossed paths like streams of water in a jet-spray fight. What had started as an ordinary childhood game had turned into a nightmare neither of them could have imagined.

Z saw his best friend bleeding. He wanted to reach him. He wanted to do something, anything, but his body refused to move.

He couldn't even reach out to Hassan.

That image stayed with him long after the gunfire stopped.

Blood and death made Z weak, but they also shaped the man he eventually became. He fell hard and continued falling. The men he grew up around had an enormous influence on him. Unfortunately, he had very few good role models to look up to. The men around him were insensitive, hardened by their surroundings, and often glorified violence and murder.

They taught him that fear was weakness, compassion was vulnerability, and violence was power.

But Z was different.

He had compassion. He had empathy. He understood suffering better than most men ever would.

He hated violence, perhaps because he understood its consequences better than anyone around him. Yet, somewhere along the way, the same boy who couldn't bear to watch his best friend bleed had become responsible for the deaths of twenty-six men.

He had anger, too.

And perhaps what made him more dangerous than the men he grew up around wasn't his hatred or his cruelty.

It was his capacity for vengeance.

**Because Z never forgot.**

And perhaps his greatest tragedy wasn't that he had become a killer.

It was that somewhere along the way, he had stopped recognising the boy who once couldn't bear to watch someone bleed.
